About the academy

Practical robotics. Real engineering.

K-Tron Robotics Academy is an independent robotics and engineering education initiative based in The Gambia. It gives young people practical experience in robotics, electronics, programming, automation and engineering problem-solving.

The academy aims to move students beyond learning about technology, and to give them opportunities to design, build, programme, test and improve real engineering projects.

Our mission

To equip young Gambians with practical engineering and technology skills while developing the creativity, confidence and technical ability needed to participate in a technology-driven world.

Our vision

To help develop a generation of Gambian innovators, engineers and robotics competitors capable of solving local problems and competing internationally.

Our approach

Learn by building, testing and fixing.

Students learn through practical activities, guided instruction, teamwork, individual projects and robotics challenges. They are encouraged to experiment, make mistakes, troubleshoot and improve their designs.

The flagship programme

Robotics & Engineering Foundation Programme

Twelve weeks of hands-on work for students aged 12–18: two sessions a week, two hours each, with an individual Arduino Uno R3 kit for every student.

Curriculum outline

Twelve weeks in four stages.

The final week-by-week plan is confirmed before the cohort starts.

  1. WEEKS1–3

    Electronics and robotics foundations

    • Electronics and circuit design basics
    • Robotics and mechanical engineering
    • First steps with the Arduino Uno R3
  2. WEEKS4–6

    Arduino programming

    • Programming the Uno R3
    • Building and testing circuits through code
    • Individual mini-projects
  3. WEEKS7–9

    Sensors, motors and automation

    • Sensors, motors and automation
    • Robot design, testing and debugging
    • Engineering teamwork and problem-solving
  4. WEEKS10–12

    FTC Java and engineering project

    • Java programming for FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC)
    • FGC robotics competition preparation
    • A final engineering project
Learning outcomes

What students can do by the end.

  • Build and test simple electronic circuits.
  • Programme an Arduino Uno R3 to read sensors and control motors.
  • Write Java for FIRST Tech Challenge robots.
  • Design, test and debug a robot, and improve it from what the tests show.
  • Work in a team and explain an engineering problem and its solution.
  • Understand what competition preparation involves.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Who can apply?

Students aged 12–18. A parent or guardian completes the application for the student.

How many places are there?

The founding cohort has six places.

What does it cost?

D5,000 per month for the 12-week programme, which is D15,000 in total. The Arduino Uno R3 starter kit is included in the fee.

Does my child keep the Arduino kit?

Yes. Each student has an individual Arduino Uno R3 starter kit that they keep permanently.

Does my child need a laptop?

Yes. Students bring their own laptop to the sessions. The academy does not supply laptops.

When and where are the sessions?

There are two two-hour sessions each week. The days, times and venue are [TO BE CONFIRMED] and will be shared with applicants.

Will my child compete internationally?

The programme introduces students to robotics competitions, including FTC and FGC-related preparation. It does not guarantee selection for any national team, delegation, registration, sponsorship, funding, visa approval or international travel. Those depend on the organisers' own rules and selection procedures.

What happens to the details we submit?

The application form does not send anything by itself. It prepares a message that you send to the academy on WhatsApp. This website does not store what you type.
